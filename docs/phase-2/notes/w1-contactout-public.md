# W1 and W5 (part): ContactOut as a visitor and its public compliance position

**Status.** Partial. Accessed 18 November 2026.
**Labels.** Observed = read directly from an official or government source. Reported = stated by a third party. Inferred = my reasoning.
**Limit.** ContactOut's own pricing, terms and privacy pages could not be read in this pass, so statements about them are Reported. Several sources are competitors or affiliates and are weighted with care.

## 1. Plans and pricing (Reported)

| Plan | Monthly | Annual billing | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Free | $0 | - | Small daily allowance; figures differ by source and date |
| Email | $49 | $39 | Capped "unlimited" |
| Email + Phone | $99 | $79 | Capped "unlimited" |
| Team / API | Quote | Quote | Seats, integrations, API |

An "Exclude US/UK Data" version of each self-serve plan is sold at about half price. Older names (Sales, Recruiter) still appear online and look outdated.

## 2. Limits and gating (Reported)

- "Unlimited" is subject to a fair-use cap of about 2,000 emails and 1,000 phone numbers a month.
- Self-serve plans, including Free, are limited to one user per company; teams are routed to a sales-led quote.
- API access is available only on the Team / API tier.
- Credit types seen in the extension (phase 1, Observed): email, phone, AI.

## 3. Data sources and legal position

- The data-subject notice names public websites and government records, customers and their users, and third-party providers, and says combined records can make the source of a field hard to identify (Reported).
- The privacy and security page uses narrower wording about crawling public emails and phones (Reported).
- Legal basis claimed: legitimate interests, GDPR Article 6(1)(f), with a reference to Article 9(2)(e) for special-category data made public by the person (Reported).
- The public terms give broad rights over data submitted by free or subsidized users, unless a premium agreement replaces them (Reported).

## 4. Opt-out and rights

- Registered as a data broker in California, approved 06/01/2022; opt-out by online request at contactout.com/optout or by email to the support address (Observed, California Attorney General registry).
- The opt-out form is described as sending a verification link and deleting the profile within 24 hours; rights requests get a response within one month (Reported).

## 5. Platform terms (Reported)

LinkedIn's User Agreement prohibits add-ons that scrape or automate its service. ContactOut markets LinkedIn lookup, bulk reveal, campaign actions and CRM or ATS handoff in its extension.

## 6. How a downstream customer documents its use (Observed)

A customer, Nova Recruiter, publishes: a privacy policy; a transparency notice under GDPR Article 14; a short Legitimate Interest Assessment (purpose, necessity, balancing); a right to object, delete, access and correct; a single contact point; and the statement that it uses a licensed provider as an independent controller.

## 7. Cross-check with phase 1

The free-tier submitted-data licence in the terms is consistent with the server-gated profile upload found in the extension (Inferred, not confirmed).

## 8. Implications for our product (Inferred)

- Pricing openings: team seats from the start, caps stated plainly, API on every plan, a regional variant that excludes sensitive markets.
- Compliance: our customers need an assessment, notice and suppression process; templates and tooling for those are a product feature.
- Ask the lawyer whether our model would make us a data broker under any law that applies to us.
